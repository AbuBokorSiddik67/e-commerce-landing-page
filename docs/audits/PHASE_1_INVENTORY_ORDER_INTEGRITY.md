## A. Phase 1 work in the current tree

- Cancellation, manual order transitions, return creation/restocking, COD collection/settlement, payment finalization and reservation expiry use a shared PostgreSQL order-row lock. Applicable local status, stock movements, reservation release, history and audit writes are transactional. Duplicate cancellation and whole-order return restocking are serialized among cooperating writers; generic status changes cannot set `CANCELLED` or `RETURNED`.
- Stock restoration and fulfillment check quantities against sale movements and product/variant relationships. Prior non-sale movements, including legacy `RELEASE` entries, conflicting dispatch history or return evidence, and uncertain financial/payment states fail closed rather than being rewritten or guessed away. No historical records were backfilled.
- Checkout locks and rechecks the product and variant before allocating stock. Product creation records opening stock in its creation transaction; publishing does not invent another opening movement. Inventory edits compare balance and movement count under a variant lock; product edits also use submitted status, stock, movement-count, timestamp and raw-attribute preconditions. Variants use one locale-independent lock ordering, including mixed-case IDs. These protections assume cooperating, append-only writers; the product status predicate is not a general product version and does not solve every product-level or status-ABA stale write.
- New-code courier booking commits a durable `PENDING` claim before provider I/O, outside the network call. New-code cancellation refuses to restore stock if **any** courier record exists; competing bookings and fulfillment fail closed on unresolved claims. Exact eligible retries of a version-4 booked claim reuse the shipment without another provider call. Its fingerprint covers relevant order, customer, item and credential-free request fields; a keyed configuration MAC binds the provider, environment, settings, stored/resolved credentials and endpoint override. Local transactions share-lock configuration, product, customer and item snapshots through claim/finalization commits. V1–V3 claims do not pass the V4 parser and require reconciliation. The request fingerprint is not a confidentiality guarantee for predictable personal data.
- A delayed or crashed provider call, malformed response, unknown outcome, conflicting finalization, changed snapshot or persistence failure remains pending for reconciliation. The finalizer is locally idempotent for identical responses and retains conflicting responses. Claims do **not** automatically expire and there is no application claim-clearing/reconciliation workflow. Aging a claim alone never authorizes a retry or stock release.
- A mocked-adapter regression exposed a process-global Pathao OAuth token reused across different accounts/environments. Pathao now obtains a token per operation; Pathao, Steadfast and REDX booking adapters use the endpoint snapshot supplied with the claim, including an explicit provider-default snapshot. This prevents the reproduced cache/endpoint mix-ups in the synthetic tests but does not verify real provider API compatibility or external idempotency.
- COD has no new expiring payment hold. Full collection, settlement, fulfillment and booking reject contradictory evidence; partial collection is rejected because its amount is not persisted. Local COD collection does not amend instructions already sent to a courier. Online initialization records a local attempt before provider I/O, retaining stock when its outcome is uncertain. Manual expiry is permission-checked, not a scheduler. Real adapters currently do not establish terminal failure of the whole payment session; synthetic terminal evidence in tests is not proof of provider behavior.
- Payment verification is outside the order transaction. Confirmation requires a matching provider-returned reference, positive exact amount/currency, provider/environment and a locked recheck of local evidence. A verified capture that cannot safely confirm is retained for reconciliation rather than reopening the order or consuming restored stock. This shared Phase 1 boundary does not constitute a completed payment integration, merchant settlement, or Phase 2 approval.

## B. Critical F1 — old and new writers can still release booked stock

The regression tests import the **byte-identical** `f151222` copies at `tests/fixtures/legacy-f151222/{orders,inventory}.ts`. The `orders.ts` blob is `fca6124ae10d1a1119598e15f65dea8d37d8f3ec` and `inventory.ts` is `16b213da6bf031168a1b9deb7fd138218f220304`, matching `git rev-parse f151222:src/services/{orders,inventory}.ts` respectively. The tests execute those old functions with current shared dependencies and a mocked provider against a verified disposable partial-schema PostgreSQL database; they are not a deployed old binary or a live-provider test.

| Safety assertion | Actual observed result |
| --- | --- |
| Old booking entered provider I/O before its first local shipment write; new cancellation ran while it was waiting. | Cancellation was **not blocked**; order became `CANCELLED`, stock rose from 1 to 2, one cancellation movement was recorded, and the old booking subsequently wrote a `BOOKED` shipment. |
| New cancellation completed first; the old booking writer then started. | The old writer was **not blocked**: it called the mocked provider once and created a shipment for the `CANCELLED` order, whose stock had already returned to 2. |
| New booking committed its `PENDING` claim and entered provider I/O; the old cancellation writer ran. | The old cancellation was **not blocked**, restored stock to 2 and wrote one cancellation movement. The new finalizer retained a `PENDING` reconciliation record after the mocked provider reported success; the external parcel could nevertheless exist while stock is resellable. |

All three are unskipped **failing safety assertions**, not expected-failure successes. The earlier new-versus-new manual/courier and cancellation races do pass, but new claims and row locks cannot make an old unclaimed request visible or force an old writer to honor the new protocol. Old callbacks, manual assignments, returns, refunds, direct SQL and other noncooperating writers also cannot be assumed fenced merely because new writers cooperate. A rollback to the old version is similarly unsafe. There is no verified cutover fence, drain, or provider reconciliation in this repository or environment.

### Minimum evidence needed for a safe cutover — not performed here

1. Establish an enforceable, independently verified fence for **all** old/noncooperating application processes and mutation paths, including background jobs, callbacks, retries, replicas and rollback. An application flag, a new-code lock, or assuming a rolling deployment has finished is not that fence.
2. Identify and reconcile all potentially accepted old provider requests, including those with no local shipment record. Revoking database/provider access or stopping a process cannot undo a request already accepted or still being processed by the provider. Until authoritative provider evidence and the fence make the outcome safe, keep every affected order allocated and block fulfillment/restoration; a timeout or elapsed timer alone is not proof of non-acceptance.
3. For new unresolved claims, account for any delayed finalizer and verify provider state before any authorized recovery. There is no safe automatic reset or supported operator-clearing workflow in this worktree; do not delete/change records or fabricate history to make the checks pass.
4. Exercise the **actual** cutover controls and rollback in an independently verified disposable environment, including both writer orderings and delayed provider outcomes. Preserve the failing safety tests until the dangerous overlap is genuinely prevented. Any real deployment controls, role/privilege changes, schema work or historical-data repair require the appropriate separate authorization and an approved non-destructive plan.

## E. Other unresolved findings and limitations

- **Provider contracts and recovery:** official [SSLCommerz v4 documentation](https://developer.sslcommerz.com/doc/v4/) describes initiation `SUCCESS` and `GatewayPageURL`, differing from the adapter's expectations; validation/currency details also need review. Official bKash [create](https://developer.bka.sh/docs/create-payment-3.md), [execute](https://developer.bka.sh/docs/execute-payment-4) and [query](https://developer.bka.sh/docs/query-payment.md) describe a tokenized flow differing from the adapter. Nagad supplies no verified amount/currency to the shared verifier and fails closed. These are source/documentation comparisons, not live tests. Actual courier contracts, account identity, delivery, idempotency and authoritative absence/cancellation evidence remain unproven. Provider response JSON is persisted; no guarantee has been established that a real provider will not echo sensitive fields.
- **Customer-facing payment truth:** `src/app/order-confirmed/page.tsx` trusts URL payment parameters and looks up an order number without validating the supplied tracking token; `payment=verified` can suppress a pending notice without database proof. `src/app/checkout/[status]/page.tsx` says no payment was taken on a cancelled URL without provider verification. Neither page was changed or browser-tested here.
- **Data and reliability:** product partial JSON updates and general stale writes, incomplete purchase-time customer/variant snapshots, non-atomic checkout duplicate-request protection and post-commit delivery without an outbox remain. There is no database uniqueness key for shipment/stock operations or active returns. Existing inconsistent orders and pending courier/refund claims require approved reconciliation, not fabricated history. Full-schema, native-engine, migration-state and actual provider compatibility remain unknown.
